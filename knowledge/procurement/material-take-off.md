# Material Take-Off (MTO)

## What is an MTO?

A **Material Take-Off (MTO)** is a quantified list of the materials
needed to build a design, extracted ("taken off") from drawings and
models. For piping, an MTO lists items such as pipe (by size, schedule,
material and length), fittings, flanges, gaskets, bolts, valves and
supports — each described using the piping class so procurement buys
exactly what the design specifies.

MTOs exist for most disciplines (piping, structural steel, electrical
cable, instrumentation bulk materials, insulation, painting).

## MTO stages

MTOs are refined as the design develops:

1. **Preliminary MTO** — from early layouts/P&IDs; used for early
   estimates and ordering long-lead or bulk items.
2. **Intermediate MTO(s)** — updated from the developing 3D model.
3. **Final MTO** — from issued-for-construction drawings/isometrics.

Each update is compared with the previous one so additional quantities can
be ordered or surplus identified.

## Allowances

Take-off quantities are usually increased by **allowances** for cutting
waste, breakage, loss, and design growth. Allowance percentages are set by
project or company procedure and differ by commodity and design stage.

## MTO vs BOM

- An **MTO** is a design-derived quantity list, often across a whole area or project, used to drive procurement.
- A **BOM (Bill of Materials)** is typically the component list for a
  specific assembly, drawing or isometric (for example the material table on a piping isometric).

## From MTO to purchase

MTO → material requisition (MR) → request for quotation (RFQ) → bid
evaluation → purchase order (PO) → expediting → receipt and inspection.

## Common MTO problems

- Descriptions that don't match the piping class.
- Double counting between MTO revisions.
- Missing small-bore items, bolts or gaskets.
- Allowances applied twice (by engineering and by procurement).
