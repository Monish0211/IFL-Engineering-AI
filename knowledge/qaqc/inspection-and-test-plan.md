# Inspection and Test Plan (ITP)

## What is an ITP?

An **Inspection and Test Plan (ITP)** is a quality document that lists,
in sequence, every inspection, test and verification activity required to
demonstrate that a product or piece of work meets its specified
requirements — and defines **who** is involved at each step and to what
extent.

ITPs are prepared by the contractor, fabricator or vendor for a specific
scope (for example: pressure vessel fabrication, piping installation,
pump manufacture) and are reviewed and approved by the client or its
representative before the work starts.

## Typical ITP columns

- Activity / inspection step, in manufacturing or construction sequence
- Reference document (drawing, specification, procedure, code)
- Acceptance criteria
- Verifying document / record (e.g. material certificate, NDT report, test report)
- Inspection involvement for each party (manufacturer, contractor, client, third party)

## Intervention points

Each party's involvement is shown with a code. Common conventions:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **H — Hold point** | Work must **not** proceed past this step until the designated party has inspected or approved it. |
| **W — Witness point** | The party is notified in advance and may attend; if they do not attend after proper notice, work may proceed. |
| **R — Review** | Documents/records are reviewed (e.g. material certificates, NDT reports). |
| **S / M — Surveillance / Monitor** | Random or periodic monitoring without formal notification. |
| **P — Perform** | The party carries out the activity. |

Exact codes and notification periods are defined in the project's quality
requirements; always use the project's own legend.

## Typical activities in a fabrication ITP

1. Review and approval of procedures (WPS/PQR, NDT, painting, test procedures)
2. Material receipt inspection and certificate review
3. Welder qualification verification
4. Fit-up and dimensional inspection
5. Welding and NDT
6. Post-weld heat treatment (where required)
7. Pressure test
8. Surface preparation and coating inspection
9. Final inspection, marking, and release note
10. Final documentation / manufacturer's record book

## Why ITPs matter

- They agree inspection scope and responsibilities **before** work starts.
- Hold points protect critical steps that cannot be verified later (e.g. before a weld is covered).
- Signed ITPs and records form part of the quality dossier at handover.
