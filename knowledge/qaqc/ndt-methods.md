# Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) Methods

NDT examines materials and welds for defects without damaging them. The
required method, extent (e.g. percentage of welds) and acceptance criteria
come from the governing code and project specification.

## Surface methods

- **VT — Visual Testing**: the first and most basic inspection; checks weld
  profile, undercut, surface porosity, cracks, dimensions.
- **PT — Liquid Penetrant Testing**: a dye penetrates surface-breaking
  defects and is drawn out by a developer. Works on non-porous materials,
  including non-magnetic stainless steels.
- **MT — Magnetic Particle Testing**: detects surface and near-surface
  defects in **ferromagnetic** materials (e.g. carbon steel) using a
  magnetic field and iron particles.

## Volumetric methods

- **RT — Radiographic Testing**: X-ray or gamma radiation produces an image
  of the weld volume; good for porosity and slag inclusions; requires
  radiation safety controls.
- **UT — Ultrasonic Testing**: high-frequency sound detects internal
  defects and measures thickness. Advanced forms include **PAUT**
  (phased array) and **TOFD** (time-of-flight diffraction).

## Other tests often listed alongside NDT

- **Hardness testing** — e.g. after PWHT or for sour-service limits.
- **PMI (Positive Material Identification)** — confirms alloy composition.
- **Pressure / leak testing** — proves strength and tightness.

## Choosing a method

| Need | Typical methods |
|---|---|
| Surface cracks, carbon steel | MT, PT |
| Surface cracks, stainless steel | PT |
| Internal defects in butt welds | RT, UT |
| Wall thickness / corrosion | UT |

NDT personnel are normally required to be qualified/certified to a
recognised scheme, as specified by the project.
