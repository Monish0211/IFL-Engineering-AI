# PFD, P&ID and HAZOP

## PFD (Process Flow Diagram)

A simplified diagram of the main process: major equipment, main process
streams and their direction, key control loops, and usually a stream table
from the **heat and material balance** (flow, temperature, pressure,
composition). It does not show every valve, line or instrument.

## P&ID (Piping and Instrumentation Diagram)

The detailed diagram used for design, construction, commissioning and
operation. A P&ID shows:

- all equipment with tag numbers,
- every line with its line number, size and piping class,
- valves (isolation, control, check, relief),
- instruments and control/shutdown functions,
- spec breaks, drains, vents, sample points and utility connections.

P&IDs are issued through revisions (for example: issued for review →
approved/for design → issued for construction) and are a primary input to
the line list, instrument index, and HAZOP.

## HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study)

A structured, team-based review of a design, usually performed on the
P&IDs. The system is divided into **nodes**; for each node, **guidewords**
(e.g. no/more/less/reverse) are combined with **parameters** (flow,
pressure, temperature, level) to generate **deviations** such as "more
pressure". For each credible deviation the team records causes,
consequences, existing safeguards, and recommendations.

HAZOP actions must be formally tracked and closed out; the resulting
design changes are reflected in the next P&ID revision.

## Related studies

- **HAZID** — early, high-level hazard identification.
- **LOPA** — semi-quantitative assessment of whether independent protection layers are sufficient.
- **SIL assessment** — determines the required safety integrity level of safety instrumented functions.
